For teachers

A ready lesson on how people around the world live, for students aged about 12 to 18. It takes 45 minutes and needs one screen at the front of the room. Everything is free to use.

1. Make your slides

Pick countries, then start. Each slide shows one country's people from poorest to richest, with one plain sentence. With guess first on, students see only the country's name until you reveal the answer, which works well for discussion.

    Start slides

    Keys: → or space for the next slide (or to reveal), ← to go back. "Full screen" is on each slide.

    2. The lesson (45 minutes)

    By the end, students can read an income distribution, explain what a median is, compare two countries in a fair way, and say why "price-adjusted" money matters.

    1. Starter, 5 min: where do you think you stand? Ask: "Out of every 100 people in the world, how many do you think live on less than you?" Students write a number down and keep it.
    2. Read one bar, 10 min. Show one country with guess first. Before revealing, ask students to sketch how they think its people spread across the coloured income levels. Reveal and compare. Explain: the bar is everyone in the country, lined up from poorest to richest; red is the bottom of the ladder, grey the top.
    3. Compare, 10 min. Show three countries from different continents (try the "Around the world" set). In pairs: which country surprised you most, and why? What would a family on $5 a day be able to buy where you live?
    4. Change over time, 10 min. On the main site, open a country such as India, China or Bangladesh and scroll to "How it has changed". What happened to the share of people in extreme poverty? Point out the note when a survey changed its method: numbers are not always comparable.
    5. Back to the starter, 5 min. Use "Where do I stand?" on the main site with a typical household income for your area (not a student's own). How close were the guesses?
    6. Exit question, 5 min. "One thing these numbers can't tell us about people's lives is…"

    Glossary

    Median
    The middle person's income: half the people live on less, half on more.
    Per person
    A household's income divided by everyone in it, children included.
    Price-adjusted dollars
    Money corrected for prices, so $1 buys the same amount everywhere. A dollar goes much further in some countries than others.
    Extreme poverty
    Living on less than $3.00 a day in price-adjusted dollars: the World Bank's line.
    Household survey
    A government study that asks thousands of households what they earn or spend. Countries run them every few years.
    Estimate
    A careful calculation, not a measurement: the World Bank moves older surveys forward to a recent year using economic growth.
