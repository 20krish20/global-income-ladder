    How to read this page

    1. ← poorestrichest →

      Each bar is everyone in a country. People are lined up from the poorest on the left to the richest on the right. A wider block means more people.

    2. Colour shows what people live on. Red is the bottom of the ladder, amber is the middle, grey is the top. Darker red is poorer.

    3. More red means more people are poor. Pick any country in the list to see its own numbers, in plain words.

      How to read the numbers

      Today's US dollars. Each survey's incomes are first brought to this year's prices using the country's own inflation, then converted at the current exchange rate. This is the money people would actually hold, in dollars.

      Local currency. The same amounts before the exchange-rate step, in the currency people are paid in. Open a country and use the switch in its panel.

      Price-adjusted dollars. A dollar buys far more in some countries than others. This view corrects for that, so the same number means roughly the same standard of living everywhere. Amounts are in international dollars.

      Re-priced, not re-surveyed. Each row shows the year people were surveyed. Amounts are updated for prices and exchange rates, but not for pay rises or growth since that survey.

      Income is per person. A household's total is divided by everyone in it, children included. A family of four on $40 a day counts as four people on $10 a day.

      Some surveys measure income, others spending. Richer countries mostly report income; most others report what households consume. Spending-based figures tend to look more equal, so compare across the two with care.

      No world total. Countries run their surveys in different years, from to . Adding them up would mix different years into one number, so each country stands on its own.
